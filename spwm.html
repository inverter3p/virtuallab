Read the waveforms

Why is pole voltage different from phase voltage?

A pole is measured to the negative DC rail. A load phase is measured to the floating star point. Subtracting the neutral voltage removes the common-mode component. Line-to-line voltage is the difference of two pole voltages.

Why does third-harmonic injection increase voltage utilization?

The same third harmonic flattens all three reference peaks. It cancels in line-to-line voltages and permits a fundamental modulation index up to 2/√3, about 15.47% above ordinary SPWM. M₁ is the fundamental reference amplitude. In peak-reference normalization, M₁ = (2/√3)Mₐ for third-harmonic injection. In fundamental normalization, M₁ = Mₐ. The injected amplitude is always M₁/6.

Peak, fundamental RMS and total PWM RMS

The cards report the fundamental component extracted from v ab. Total RMS of the switched waveform includes harmonics and is generally larger. The ideal linear formula applies only before reference clipping; harmonic injection cancels at the line terminals only while the modulation remains linear.

Suggested comparisons

1. Compare bipolar and unipolar H-bridge modulation at the same Mₐ, VDC and fc. Observe the zero-voltage states and switching sidebands.

2. Use peak-reference normalization and select Mₐ = 1 for both SPWM and third-harmonic injection. Compare fundamental RMS and reference peaks.

3. Increase Mₐ beyond the linear limit. Observe flat-topped average voltage and low-order harmonics.

4. Use a three-carrier-period window to follow comparator crossings → gate states → pole voltages → line voltages.
